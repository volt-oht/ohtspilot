Revision 002: conditional moving-lead acceleration allowance

Status: IMPLEMENTED, DISABLED. No installed driving-controller change yet.
Activation awaits the device restart clarification and a guarded deployment.
This is a limited experimental candidate, not vehicle safety acceptance.

Purpose
When an already moving Volt follows a radar lead that is pulling away with
surplus distance, permit up to 0.3 m/s^2 above the original planning ceiling,
without exceeding 1.4 m/s^2. The MPC still chooses acceleration; this allowance
is not a direct acceleration command and does not guarantee faster movement.

Eligibility
- Volt fingerprint and openpilot longitudinal control only.
- Active PID operation, speed above 2 m/s and at most 80 km/h.
- Fresh valid radar, no radarErrors, no pedals or standstill/forceDecel.
- Both measured and planned acceleration must be nonnegative.
- Every present lead must be radar-based, nondecelerating, and pulling away
  by more than 0.5 m/s. LeadOne must be present.
- Distance must exceed stopDistance + selected GAP time * speed by over 2 m.
  The allowance grows continuously to its maximum over 2..8 m surplus and
  0.5..2 m/s relative speed. This formula is an eligibility reference, not a
  certified safe distance.
- Disabled when existing dynamic-cost or signal-stop experiments are active.
- Existing turn acceleration limits still apply after this allowance.

Scope
No changes to GAP targets, stopping/starting state transitions, brake or gas
CAN mapping, safety firmware, braking limits or stoppingDecelRate. Therefore
this revision does not fix standstill release or final-stop discomfort.

Validation
8 helper tests and 5 actual planner-update input tests pass. Input tests use
a recording MPC, not fake optimization output. Native planner import and
construction with recorded vehicle CarParams pass on LEPRO.
The installed acados solver was used for 6 original/candidate comparisons:
pull-away, acceleration then braking, stationary lead, close lead, temporary
lead loss, and a second restrictive lead. Vehicle motion was an ideal plant;
the Volt actuators, PID, sensing delay and road conditions were not simulated.
No solver errors in these scenarios. Stationary and close-lead trajectories
were identical. All modeled primary-lead minimum gaps were >=4 m; this is
only a test observation, not proof of safety in other situations.

Pull-away speed at 10 s: 18.5347377 -> 18.6092304 m/s (+0.268 km/h).
Acceleration then braking peak |jerk|: 1.3924361 -> 1.4144815 m/s^3 (+1.6%).
The latter minimum primary-lead gap: 4.5087469 -> 4.5033219 m.
See 002_native_summary.json for all cases. Comfort improvement is unproven.
The cost-only precursor was rejected for negligible speed benefit.

Recorded-input screening (not a new trajectory or behavior replay)
Assuming dynamic cost OFF, 71 of 746 aligned samples in the nonintervention
segment met the allowance conditions; the largest allowance was 0.129 m/s^2.
The recorded dynamic-cost setting is uncertain; do not claim reproduction.
